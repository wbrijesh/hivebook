= Product Principles
:status: stable
:last-reviewed: 2026-06-06

The load-bearing commitments behind every design decision. Appealable
in writing, not in passing.

. *Passive ingestion, not user feeding.* Knowledge enters by connecting
the systems where it already lives. If something requires a human to
remember to log it, it is out of scope.
. *The corpus must be legible.* The structured form — the book index —
is human-readable, browsable, and auditable. A new employee can read
it. Hivebook is not a black box of embeddings.
. *Every claim cites its source.* Summaries cite specific evidence, per
the hierarchy invariant (xref:ROOT:adrs/0003-hierarchy-invariant.adoc[ADR-0003]). Trust and stale-detection both
depend on it.
. *Access is admin-controlled.* Admins assign chapter-level access
directly or via templates. The system does not infer access from
source-system ACLs (xref:ROOT:adrs/0009-chapter-level-access.adoc[ADR-0009]).
. *Per-customer isolation is absolute.* No content crosses customers,
and no cross-customer signal is shared without explicit opt-in
(xref:ROOT:adrs/0010-day-zero-enterprise-primitives.adoc[ADR-0010]).

== Non-goals

* *No graph database.* Cross-references are flat related-entry links.
Multi-hop graph reasoning is out of scope.
* *No freeform editing of summaries.* Admins reorganize structurally
(move entries) but do not author or edit summary prose. Summaries come
from evidence.
* *No structural creation by admins.* Splits, merges, and new entries
are system-quality decisions made by the filing pipeline and
maintenance loop, not by users.
* *Not a chatbot.* Conversational Q&A may be an interface; the product
is the structured corpus.
* *Not a search engine.* Search exists internally but is not the
primary user surface.
