= Audience
:status: draft
:last-reviewed: 2026-06-06

Hivebook is sold business-to-business. The buyer is a company, not an
individual. It:

* has fragmented operational knowledge across SaaS tools and human heads
* runs, or wants to run, AI agents that need that knowledge
* has the security and procurement posture of a real business — an
enterprise sales motion, not credit-card self-serve

== Open question: mid-market vs. true enterprise for the first 18 months

These are different products in practice. The decision changes storage
architecture, deployment model, and sales motion, and is needed before
the architecture and roadmap can be made concrete.

[width="100%",cols="21%,39%,40%",options="header",]
|===
|Dimension |Mid-market (200–2k employees) |True enterprise (5k{plus})
|Deployment |SaaS only |SaaS, VPC, or on-prem inference
|Storage |Single multi-tenant region |Customer-pinned region, BYO storage
|Auth |SSO (SAML/OIDC) {plus} SCIM |Plus fine-grained policy engine, CMK
|Sales cycle |Weeks |Quarters
|Procurement bar |Security questionnaire |Pen-test reports, SOC2/ISO, DPIA, MSAs
|Per-customer ARR |$20k–150k |$250k{plus}
|===

Until this is decided, the architecture leaves the door open to true
enterprise — tenant isolation, residency tagging, and BYOK-ready
encryption are day-zero (xref:ROOT:adrs/0010-day-zero-enterprise-primitives.adoc[ADR-0010]) — without requiring the full
enterprise stack on day one.
