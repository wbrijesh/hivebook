== Product Principles

These are the load-bearing commitments behind every design
decision. They are appealable in writing, not in passing.

=== 1. Passive ingestion, not user feeding

Knowledge enters Trenches by being connected to the systems where
it already lives — not by users typing it in. If something requires
a human to remember to log it, it is not in scope.

=== 2. The corpus must be legible

Trenches is not a black box of embeddings. The structured form of
the company’s knowledge — the book index — is human-readable,
human-browsable, and human-auditable. A new employee should be able
to read it.

=== 3. Every claim cites its source

Summaries cite specific evidence. Sub-topic summaries cite source
spans in raw artifacts; topic summaries cite child sub-topic
summaries; chapter summaries cite child topic summaries. Not "`this
is in the corpus somewhere.`" Trust and stale-detection both depend
on this.

=== 4. Access is admin-controlled

Admins assign chapter-level access to users, directly or via access
templates representing personas or departments. The system does not
infer access from source-system ACLs and does not propagate source
permissions through derived summaries.

=== 5. Per-customer isolation is absolute

No content from one customer is ever visible to another, and no
cross-customer signal (e.g., learned patterns) is shared without
explicit opt-in.

=== Non-goals

* *No graph database.* Cross-references between entries are
stored as flat related-entry links. Multi-hop graph reasoning is
out of scope.
* *No freeform editing of summaries.* Admins can reorganize
structurally (move entries) but cannot author or edit summary
prose. Summaries come from evidence, generated and refreshed by
the system.
* *No structural creation by admins.* Splits, merges, and new
entry creation are system-quality decisions made by the filing
pipeline and maintenance loop, not by users.
* *Not a chatbot.* Conversational Q&A may be an interface, but
the product is the structured corpus.
* *Not a search engine.* Search exists internally but is not the
primary user surface.
